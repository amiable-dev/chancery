# Evidence Receipt

## Definition
An **evidence receipt** is a compact, structured, redacted record attached to an accepted change that lets a person reconstruct *why* the change was approved — containing the task, the actors involved, software versions, the assets changed, which checks ran and their results, who approved it and under what authority, provenance information, and rollback status. It is explicitly distinguished from a raw transcript: a transcript is noisy, unstructured, and may contain secrets; a receipt is a purpose-built artifact designed to support a single acceptance decision.

## Explanation
As AI agents produce a growing share of merged code, the traditional evidence that a change was safe to ship — "a senior engineer read it and a test suite passed" — stops being reconstructable after the fact. Logs pile up, chat transcripts between agents are enormous and full of noise, and the actual reasoning that led to acceptance is scattered across tool calls, review comments, and approval clicks that no one has assembled into a coherent record.

The evidence receipt is the answer: rather than treating "we have logs somewhere" as sufficient, the pipeline is designed to *produce* a specific, minimal artifact at the moment of acceptance. Anthropic frames this as the actual success metric for the pipeline — not lines of code generated, not review comments left, not tasks started, but **an accepted, safe change with a reconstructable receipt.**

**What distinguishes a receipt from a transcript:**
- **Structured** — fixed fields (task, actors, versions, changed assets, checks run, findings, approvals, provenance, rollback status), not free text.
- **Redacted** — secrets, credentials, and sensitive data are stripped before the receipt is stored; a transcript that still contains a leaked API key is not fit to keep as an audit artifact.
- **Decision-oriented** — designed to answer "why was this accepted?" for a specific change, not to be a general-purpose debugging log.
- **Reconstructable** — a person with no other context should be able to read the receipt and understand the chain from proposal to acceptance without needing to replay the agent's full working session.

**Where the fields come from:** each layer of a [[risk-tiered-code-review]] pipeline and [[separation-of-duties-agentic-sdlc]] contributes its own piece — the creating identity and task description, the deterministic check results, the narrow AI reviewer's specific claims and evidence, the human approver's identity and authority, the deploying identity's provenance record, and (if applicable) a rollback rehearsal result.

**Why this matters beyond compliance theater:** without a receipt, "the review passed" is an assertion, not a fact anyone can check later. When an incident occurs, the receipt is what turns "something went wrong somewhere in this pipeline" into "here is exactly what was checked, by whom, and on what basis it was approved" — closing what the vault separately calls the [[agent-audit-gap]].

## Key Properties
- **Structured, not narrative** — fixed schema of fields, purpose-built for reconstruction rather than general logging.
- **Redacted by design** — secrets and sensitive content are removed before the receipt is persisted, unlike a raw transcript.
- **Per-change, not per-session** — one receipt corresponds to one accepted change, not an entire agent working session (which may span many changes, dead ends, and retries).
- **The actual success metric** — Anthropic explicitly names the reconstructable receipt on an accepted safe change as the pipeline's measure of success, deliberately displacing throughput metrics (lines generated, tasks started) that don't indicate safety.
- **Composable from pipeline stages** — assembled from the outputs of separation-of-duties roles and risk-tiered review layers rather than generated by a single step.

## Relationships
- Is the audit output of [[separation-of-duties-agentic-sdlc]]: each of the four jobs (create/check/authorize/deploy) contributes an identifiable piece of the receipt.
- Assembled from the layers of [[risk-tiered-code-review]]: deterministic check results, narrow AI reviewer claims, and human approval records are receipt components.
- Closes the [[agent-audit-gap]]: the audit gap is the *absence* of a tamper-proof, reconstructable record of agent action; the evidence receipt is the concrete artifact that fills that absence for code changes specifically.
- Related to [[agent-attestation-standards]]: attestation is the cryptographic, machine-verifiable evolution of the same idea — binding a commit to signed proof of agent/model/authorizer identity, whereas a receipt is (initially) a structured but not necessarily cryptographically-signed record. Receipts are a practical precursor to full attestation.
- Related to [[siem-integrated-ai-governance]]: receipts and audit events are natural inputs to a SIEM pipeline — the receipt is the durable per-change record; SIEM integration is the streaming/alerting layer that consumes such events in near-real-time.
- Contrasts with a raw transcript: the receipt is explicitly *not* "just keep all the logs" — it is a deliberate reduction and redaction of the underlying activity into a decision-support artifact.

## Applications
- **Pipeline design:** Build a receipt-generation step into the acceptance/merge path itself, so producing the receipt is not an afterthought bolted on for compliance but a required part of accepting the change.
- **Incident investigation:** When something in production traces back to an AI-authored change, pull the receipt first — it should answer who created it, what was checked, who approved it, and under what reasoning, without needing to replay agent sessions.
- **Metric selection:** When evaluating whether an agentic SDLC pipeline is working, resist throughput metrics (PRs merged, lines generated) in favor of receipt-completeness and acceptance-safety metrics.
- **Redaction policy:** When designing what goes into a receipt, explicitly define what must be stripped (credentials, PII, raw prompts containing sensitive context) before the receipt is stored — treat this as a required step, not a nice-to-have.
