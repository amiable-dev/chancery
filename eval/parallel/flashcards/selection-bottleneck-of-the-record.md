# Selection as the bottleneck of the record — Flashcards

#flashcards/knowledge-management

## Definition <!-- kb:card:bc9b04 -->
What is the "selection as the bottleneck of the record" diagnosis?
?
The limit on using accumulated knowledge is not producing or storing it but consulting it — the record grows cheaply and without bound while selection is the slow, binding step.

## Named cause <!-- kb:card:2436a5 -->
What does the essay name as the cause of the selection bottleneck?
?
Artificial indexing — an item sits in only one place, is found only by descending from subclass to subclass, and the path rules to do so are cumbersome to learn.

## Human recall vs. indexed filing <!-- kb:card:7c7219 -->
How does human recall differ mechanically from indexed filing, per the essay?
?
Recall works by association — one item in mind snaps to the next along a web of trails — rather than requiring the searcher to exit the system and re-enter on a fresh path for each new item.

## Two selection strategies <!-- kb:card:823fc2 -->
What are the two mechanical selection strategies the essay distinguishes?
?
Simple selection, which examines every item in a set for specified characteristics, and class-narrowing selection (like an automatic telephone exchange), which attends only to a narrowing class and never touches most of the collection.

## Why the diagnosis outlived the tech <!-- kb:card:24f51e -->
Why does the diagnosis still hold even though the essay's own technology (microfilm, photocells) is obsolete?
?
Every retrieval system since — catalogs, search engines, wikis — has only moved where the bottleneck sits, without dislodging the claim that consultation, not accumulation, constrains use of the record.
