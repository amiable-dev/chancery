# Answer-layer interception — Flashcards

#flashcards/search

## Definition: answer-layer interception <!-- kb:card:00bd82 -->
What is answer-layer interception, and what does it make the operative visibility goal?
?
The condition where a majority of an audience forms its impression from a machine-generated summary placed above results and never reaches the source — making representation inside the generated answer, not ranking beneath it, the operative visibility goal.

## Mechanism: position and sufficiency <!-- kb:card:0cc1d7 -->
Why does the summary's position make the underlying click optional for many questions?
?
The summary is read first and is good enough for a large class of questions, so the reader never needs to click through — and whichever sources the generator drew on are the only ones the reader ever encounters.

## Mechanism: channel migration <!-- kb:card:35d55b -->
What second mechanism, besides summary position, shifts visibility away from ranked results?
?
Channel migration — a growing share of questions are asked directly in an assistant, where there is no results page or ranked list to appear on at all.

## Evidence caveat: self-reported, not observed <!-- kb:card:6714f4 -->
What kind of evidence underlies answer-layer interception's claims, and what is that evidence's key limitation?
?
Self-reported, US-only, cross-sectional survey data, not observed click-stream measurement — so reading a summary is not the same as forgoing the click, and adoption figures cannot be read as behavioral proof.

## Adoption is not trust <!-- kb:card:08cfea -->
Why does the concept caution against reading adoption of AI summaries as endorsement?
?
The same survey population reporting heavy use also reports concern — about seven in ten expected AI to worsen their data security, against only three in ten reporting productivity gains.
