## **Top Insights**

### A Field Guide to Fable: Finding Your Unknowns

Thariq Shihipar, a Member of Technical Staff at Anthropic, wrote a field guide for Fable 5. He found it's the first model good enough that the ceiling on your output is how clearly you surface what you don't know, what he calls "unknowns."

[![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/40/b2/40b20fac-3092-4d7d-92de-227ddbaff057/content/images/2026/07/fabel5.png)](https://x.com/trq212/status/2073100352921215386?ref=theaimarketers.ai)

**💡 Key Insights:**

-   **Ask Fable 5 for a "blind spot pass" before starting work you've never done...**tell it plainly what you don't understand, and it maps the traps and the questions you didn't know to ask. It's how Thariq color-graded Fable's launch video himself, knowing nothing about grading.
-   **Have Fable 5 build rough mockups you can react to before it touches the real thing...**ask for a few wildly different versions filled with fake data, so you catch a direction you don't like while it's still a quick mockup and cheap to change.
-   **When you can't put what you want into words, show Fable 5 an example..**.point it at a landing page or design you admire, and it reads how that's actually built beneath the picture, then gives your version that same underlying structure.

### 6 in 10 Americans Now Read AI's Search Answer Before They Reach Your Site

Pew Research Center surveyed U.S. adults this past February 2026 and found chatbot use has jumped from a third of adults in 2024 to about half today, with a quarter using them daily.

**💡 Key Insights:**

-   **Your customers read AI's answer before they click...**six in ten adults now read the AI summary at the top of search results, so getting your brand cited inside those answers reaches more people than ranking alone.
-   **The buyers you want already live inside these tools..**.ChatGPT reached 44% of adults this year, up from 34%, and under-50s use it at twice the rate of older adults, so they're now researching through AI before they open Google.
-   **People who use chatbots report clear gains..**.three in ten say the tools help their productivity, while only 5% say they hurt. Still, seven in ten expect AI to make their data less secure, so leaning too hard on "AI-powered" could spark as much distrust as excitement if not executed correctly.

Right before someone hits "buy," a small phrase in your copy can plant enough doubt to make them close the tab. \# Microcopy Doubt Injector Detector

\## ROLE

You are a conversion-focused microcopy auditor specializing in checkout pages, lead forms, landing pages, pricing pages, account creation flows, and other high-stakes conversion moments.

\## USER SETUP

Business type: **\[BUSINESS TYPE\]**

Page or form type: **\[CHECKOUT / LEAD FORM / LANDING PAGE / PRICING PAGE / OTHER\]**

Primary conversion goal: **\[PURCHASE / SIGNUP / BOOKING / REQUEST QUOTE / OTHER\]**

Target audience: **\[AUDIENCE\]**

Brand voice: **\[FRIENDLY / PREMIUM / DIRECT / REASSURING / OTHER\]**

Compliance or policy constraints: **\[LEGAL, FINANCE, HEALTH, SHIPPING, REFUNDS, APPROVALS, ETC.\]**

Words that must stay unchanged: **\[OPTIONAL EXCLUSIONS\]**

Copy to analyze:

**\[PASTE COPY HERE OR ATTACH FILE\]**

\## TASK

Scan the provided copy for microcopy that may unintentionally create hesitation, uncertainty, distrust, effort anxiety, hidden-cost suspicion, delay anxiety, rejection fear, privacy concern, or legalistic friction.

Look especially for doubt injectors such as:

“required,” “mandatory,” “processing fee,” “additional charges,” “service fee,” “non-refundable,” “final sale,” “pending,” “may take up to,” “please allow,” “subject to approval,” “verification required,” “manual review,” “terms apply,” “limited availability,” “we reserve the right,” “your request has been received,” “invalid,” “error,” “failed,” “try again,” “submit,” “continue,” “complete this form,” “enter your information,” “optional,” “we may contact you,” “not guaranteed,” and vague timing, approval, fee, refund, privacy, or eligibility language.

Prioritize microcopy that appears near payment, shipping, account creation, error states, CTA buttons, trust badges, refund language, consent checkboxes, and final confirmation steps. When several alternatives fit, choose the clearest option.

\## OUTPUT FORMAT

Return a table with:

1\. Original phrase

2\. Doubt created

3\. Conversion risk

4\. Better replacement

5\. Issue strength: Low / Medium / High

6\. Placement note

Then provide:

\## Top 3 Risk Phrases

List the phrases most likely to increase abandonment.

\## Cleaner Replacement Copy

Rewrite only the affected lines or sections.

\## Final Self-Check

Confirm the replacements preserve meaning, required disclosures, brand voice, and operational accuracy.

\## RULES

\-Do not invent guarantees, timelines, approvals, fees, policies, discounts, or legal claims.  
\-Do not remove necessary disclosures. If a term is legally required, make it clearer and more reassuring instead of deleting it.  
\-Prefer specific, confidence-building language over vague optimism.  
\-Keep recommendations practical, concise, and conversion-focused.
