# Cognitive Offloading — Flashcards

#flashcards/cognitive-offloading


## Definition <!-- kb:card:19b86c -->
What is cognitive offloading?
?
The deliberate or habitual act of reducing the mental processing requirements of a task by delegating cognitive work to the external environment — using tools, systems, or physical actions (notebooks, smartphones, AI assistants) in place of internal mental effort.

## Scope <!-- kb:card:592f6d -->
What is the difference between beneficial cognitive offloading and potentially harmful cognitive offloading?
?
Beneficial offloading stores *results* externally (shopping lists, reminders) while you retain the underlying cognitive skill. Harmful offloading outsources the *process itself* (reasoning, writing, analysis) — if you never exercise the skill, it may atrophy through disuse.

## Historical Parallel <!-- kb:card:6877b8 -->
Name two historical examples of cognitive offloading that preceded AI.
?
1. Writing / literacy — offloads memory to an external medium
2. GPS navigation — offloads spatial reasoning and route planning
Others: calculators (arithmetic), search engines (recall/retrieval)

## Relationship to AI <!-- kb:card:5185b7 -->
How does AI change the nature of cognitive offloading compared to earlier tools?
?
Earlier tools offloaded specific, narrow skills (arithmetic, navigation, memorisation). AI offloads *reasoning, writing, and analysis simultaneously* — the higher-order cognitive processes that previously couldn't be delegated. This breadth is why the stakes are different.

## Extended Mind <!-- kb:card:a1948a -->
What philosophical theory underlies cognitive offloading?
?
The Extended Mind thesis (Clark & Chalmers, 1998): cognition legitimately extends beyond the brain into the environment and tools. The mind isn't bounded by the skull — it incorporates the notebooks, devices, and systems we use to think.

## Design Implication <!-- kb:card:9bc2ed -->
How should AI tools be designed to minimise harmful cognitive offloading?
?
Design for *scaffolding* rather than *replacement*: prompt users to reason first, then compare with AI output; use AI as a check or collaborator rather than a first-pass generator. Keep the human in the cognitive loop.
