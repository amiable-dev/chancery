# Neural Dimming — Flashcards

#flashcards/neural-dimming


## Definition <!-- kb:card:7bd1f9 -->
What is neural dimming?
?
The measurable reduction in brain neural activity — observed via EEG as decreased connectivity across memory, creativity, and analytical regions — when a person delegates cognitive work to AI. The brain effectively "stands down" when AI handles the thinking.

## Evidence <!-- kb:card:9a3b8d -->
What did the MIT EEG study find about neural activity levels across writing conditions?
?
Clear hierarchy of neural engagement (strongest to weakest):
1. **Brain-only writers** — strongest, most distributed connectivity
2. **Search-engine users** — moderate connectivity
3. **LLM (AI) users** — weakest connectivity across all brain regions

When LLM users later switched to unaided writing, their connectivity was still lower than the baseline brain-only group — suggesting accumulated reduction.

## Mechanism <!-- kb:card:116127 -->
Why does the brain dim when using AI?
?
The brain is efficient — it doesn't expend energy on work it doesn't need to do. When AI handles the reasoning, the neural processing that would normally occur (memory retrieval, analytical synthesis, creative generation) simply doesn't happen. The brain responds to the training stimulus it receives; habitual reduced stimulus may reset baseline engagement.

## Contrast <!-- kb:card:da00fa -->
How is neural dimming different from cognitive fatigue?
?
Neural dimming is caused by *under-engagement* — the brain not working enough. Cognitive fatigue is caused by *over-engagement* — the brain working too hard. Neural dimming is the brain standing down; fatigue is the brain burning out. Opposite causes, similar-looking EEG signatures of reduced output.

## Recall Effect <!-- kb:card:363200 -->
Why do AI-assisted writers struggle to recall their own work?
?
Because material enters long-term memory through the *process* of engaging with it — forming sentences, wrestling with ideas, making choices. When AI bypasses this process, there's no encoding. The content was never deeply processed by the human brain, so it doesn't stick. High-quality output, low retention.

## Design Response <!-- kb:card:97784a -->
How should interaction design respond to the risk of neural dimming?
?
Prompt active cognitive engagement before and after AI assistance:
- Ask users to predict or reason through a problem before seeing AI output
- Prompt users to evaluate and critique AI output rather than accept it
- Use AI responses as inputs to human reasoning, not as finished products
- Periodic "AI-free" practice as deliberate cognitive exercise
