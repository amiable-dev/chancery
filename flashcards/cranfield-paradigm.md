# Cranfield paradigm — Flashcards

#flashcards/evaluation

## Cranfield paradigm: definition <!-- kb:card:da8968 -->
What three fixed elements define the Cranfield paradigm's evaluation design?
?
A fixed document collection, a fixed query set, and per-query relevance judgments — enabling comparable, reusable evaluation.

## Cranfield 1 finding <!-- kb:card:8bd747 -->
What did the Cranfield 1 experiment find about implementation detail versus methodology?
?
One indexing method moved from least to most efficient after only minor changes to how data was arranged on the cards — implementation detail dominated the measured methodology delta.

## Gull study disagreement <!-- kb:card:4f1acb -->
In the predecessor Gull study, what fraction of relevance calls did each of two expert judge groups reject from the other?
?
Over 30%.

## Why judgments are fixed up front <!-- kb:card:230f44 -->
Why do Cranfield-paradigm evaluations fix relevance judgments per query up front rather than adjudicating after the fact?
?
Because the Gull study showed relevance judgment isn't self-evident ground truth — expert judges disagreed by 30%+, so judgments must be fixed before systems are compared.

## Historical lineage <!-- kb:card:2c0299 -->
What is the Cranfield paradigm's lineage, from founding vision to institutionalization?
?
Vannevar Bush's memex framing, to Cranfield's experiments, to TREC's institutionalization.
