# Associative trail — Flashcards

#flashcards/knowledge-management

## Definition: associative trail <!-- kb:card:13ad4f -->
What is an associative trail, and how is it built?
?
A named, persistent path through a collection, built by joining items in pairs — the user brings two items into view together, names the trail, and ties them with a mutual code written into both so either can summon the other instantly.

## Mechanism: the symmetric join <!-- kb:card:291a6e -->
Where does the join's code live, and why does that make the link belong to the items rather than a catalog?
?
In blank code spaces on each of the two items, each recording the other's index number — so the link is traversable from either end and belongs to the items themselves, not to a central catalog.

## Trails are a by-product, not a plan <!-- kb:card:f1848a -->
How are associative trails built, according to the concept?
?
While working, not planned in advance — a path is a by-product of the inquiry itself (e.g. comparing bows, branching onto elasticity when materials turn out to matter), not a separate cataloguing task.

## Trails compose rather than partition <!-- kb:card:def297 -->
How can one item participate in a collection's organization under this scheme, compared to a filing system?
?
One item can sit on many trails at once, so paths compose rather than partition the collection the way a single filing scheme would.

## A trail as a transferable artifact <!-- kb:card:13a0fd -->
What becomes possible once a trail can be copied whole into another person's store, per the original proposal?
?
A profession of 'trail blazers' who establish useful paths through the common record, and an inheritance from a teacher that includes the whole scaffolding of reasoning behind their conclusions, not just the conclusions themselves.

## Origin: a 1945 mechanical proposal <!-- kb:card:0115b2 -->
What was the associative trail's original proposed mechanism, and what is it now considered the ancestor of?
?
A 1945 proposal (Vannevar Bush's memex) built on microfilm and photocells — it is the direct ancestor of hypertext and of every linked-note system since.
